HOUSE RESOLUTION

 
2    WHEREAS, The members of the Illinois House of 
3Representatives are saddened to learn of the death of Reverend 
4Dr. Eddie R. Williams Jr.; and
 
5    WHEREAS, Dr. Williams, the son of Reverend E.R. Williams 
6Sr. and Anna Maud Williams, was born in Chicago on January 6, 
71945; he committed to his faith at an early age; he graduated 
8from the University of Chicago Laboratory Schools and then 
9attended Ottawa University, where he earned the highest honors 
10in mathematics, religion, and philosophy; he received his 
11doctorate from Columbia University in New York; he attended 
12Union Theological Seminary; he married Shirley Ann King in 
131969; and
 
14    WHEREAS, Dr. Williams was ordained by his father, the 
15pastor of South Park Baptist Church in Chicago, in 1968; he 
16served as assistant pastor from 1972 until his father's 
17passing in 1997, when he assumed the role of pastor and later 
18pastor emeritus; and
 
19    WHEREAS, Dr. Williams moved to DeKalb to join the faculty 
20at Northern Illinois University (NIU) as an assistant 
21professor of mathematics in 1970; he received tenure in the 
22Department of Mathematics in 1976 and was promoted to

3    WHEREAS, Dr. Williams transitioned to a full-time 
4administrative role when he was named associate director of 
5the NIU Office of Budget and Planning in 1980; he earned 
6several promotions, including to the roles of assistant vice 
7president for administrative affairs, director of budget and 
8planning, and vice president of finance and planning, before 
9becoming the university's first African American vice 
10president with his appointment as chief of operations and 
11executive vice president of finance and facilities in 2000; he 
12oversaw the development of 21 new buildings and facilities as 
13well as seven major building redesigns and renovations, 
14including projects such as Barsema Hall, the Chilled Water 
15Plant, and the development of three satellite educational 
16centers in Rockford, Naperville, and Hoffman Estates; and
 
17    WHEREAS, Dr. Williams was a passionate advocate for 
18students and dedicated himself to the NIU community, providing 
19leadership, guidance, and support; he was a champion of the 
20Center for Black Studies, the Martin Luther King Jr. Commons, 
21and the McKinley "Deacon" Davis CHANCE Program; affectionately 
22known as "Doc Eddie", he was a devoted fan of the Huskies and 
23member of the "NBA", or Noontime Basketball Association; he 
24was the faculty adviser of NIU's Northern Black Choir for 35
